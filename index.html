Remina Greenfield

I am a New York–based cultural strategist and creative producer. I lead international exhibitions and public commissions and bring artists, scientists, and engineers together to give experimental ideas a public life. A question runs through my work as a producer, curator, and writer: what can people discover through an encounter with an artwork that they could never learn from a description of it?

As Senior Project Manager at Tomokazu Matsuyama Studio, I lead five project managers delivering exhibitions, public artworks, and collaborations. Previously, over eight years at Anicka Yi Studio, I advanced from Director of Research to Studio Director. I guided research and production across works ranging from kelp sculptures to paintings and moving-image works developed through machine learning. I oversaw a seven-figure studio operating budget and production budgets exceeding $3 million.

For Yi’s Turbine Hall commission at Tate Modern, I recruited specialists in robotics, software, and indoor positioning and coordinated test flights for a group of autonomous flying sculptures. We used heat signatures so the machines could respond to the crowd while preserving each visitor’s anonymity. I later co-wrote Yi’s TED talk around a live flight and led the sculptures’ return to public view at the New Museum.

I co-developed Metaspore, Yi’s nomadic research initiative, organizing salons, symposia, publications, and a course and accompanying exhibition at Stanford University. My curatorial collaborations with Pirelli HangarBicocca, NEW INC, and Pratt explore how other organisms sense the world and how those ways of sensing might change our own.

I studied art and biology at St. Mary’s College of Maryland and earned an MFA in Design and Technology at Parsons School of Design, where I have also designed and taught courses. My writing moves between criticism and fiction. In “Quiet Enjoyment,” my short story for Counterfictional Futures, a woman leases her body to a biotechnology company while secretly harboring a microbial colony descended from her mother’s sourdough starter.
